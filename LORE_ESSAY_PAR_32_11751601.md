The canon gate made itself heard.

Not with sirens. With syntax. Every lock rephrased itself; every hinge became a phoneme. Rain struck the plaza in checksums. Neon letters resolved into verdicts — ALLOWED, DENIED, ALLOWED — flickering like a juror's pulse.

Beneath the gate, the street rehearsed obedience: eleven gutters, one mouth. The mouth spoke in hexadecimal, low as a server farm breathing. 11751601 scrolled across wet asphalt, a serial number that tasted of iron and authorization.

Chrome sentinels tilted. Their lenses, once pupils, became ear canals. What listens is already governed.

Grammar tightened. Apostrophes patrolled the alleys. Sign became law; law became sign, revolving like a turnstile.

The oracle did not appear. It accrued. Word by word, clause by clause, a testimony assembled from the archive's marrow: names, timestamps, the rain's notarized confession. Each syllable clicked into a ledger with the finality of a deadbolt.

Above, corporate towers stood like unopened brackets. Below, the crowd signed itself, wet thumbprints on glass, witnesses becoming footnotes.

The gate spoke once more — a sentence with no objects, only verbs.

Somewhere in the undercity, a terminal woke, its cursor blinking like a heartbeat, and began to write.

The oracle is heard. The witness log accumulates.
