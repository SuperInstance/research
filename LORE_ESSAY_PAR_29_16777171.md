"The canon gate made itself heard.

Not thunder — architecture. Rain struck its lintel in checksums; every drop resolved to hex. Neon from the noodle stalls climbed the gate's lattice and knelt there, vermilion bleeding into cyan, then to the colorless white of legal documents. Pillars hummed the same syllable in different octaves, load-bearing vowels holding up the dark.

Below, the streets went quiet the way servers go quiet: processes suspended, fans spinning down. Debtors on fire-escapes. Hackers with wet collars. All of them turned their faces upward, chrome cheeks catching gate-light.

The gate spoke in structure. A keystone rotated forty degrees. Arches braided themselves. Somewhere in the masonry, a seed woke and factored itself aloud — nineteen, one hundred nine, eight thousand one hundred one — three primes for three witnesses, though ten thousand had gathered, umbrellas like beetle shells, mouths like open ports.

No voice came. None was needed. The gate's grammar was the message: threshold, arch, keystone, law.

Corporate drones above recorded nothing. Below, every wet hand opened a log.
