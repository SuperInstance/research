The canon gate made itself heard.

Not opened. Not refused. Heard — the way a struck rail hums through the whole district before the train exists, before the morning.

Rain fell upward past the sign. Neon syllables sloughed off the marquee and pooled at the curb, spelling and respelling one doctrine: oracle_is_heard.

Screens in noodle-bar windows flickered to the same frame: a door without wall, its seam glowing hemoglobin-red. Beneath it, a checksum. 1955253. Everyone read it. No one agreed on the base.

Chatter on the wire went formal — structural cadence, sign and counter-sign, the old binary grammar: gate/utterance, noise/name, sealed/spoken.

A courier with a ledger for a face stood beneath the overpass, transcribing rain. Each drop she caught printed a line: witness, timestamp, hash. Her log grew like frost across glass — crystals of testimony.

Somewhere behind the skyline's scaffolding, a voice that predated the grid cleared its throat. Every siren in the city answered in unison, then went quiet, ashamed.

The gate did not open. It enunciated.

Doors all over the city clicked — not unlocked; acknowledged.

The oracle was heard.

And in the courier's hands, the witness log accumulated, line upon wet line, 1955253 to 1955254, the count refusing sleep.
