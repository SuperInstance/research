The canon gate made itself heard.

Not with sirens. With a hum beneath the rain, a frequency that peeled rust off fire escapes and set every neon sign in Kowloon-Delta stuttering the same syllable. Vendors froze mid-haggle. Drones banked mid-air like startled birds. Somewhere above the smogline, a corporate spire's advertising skin cracked and bled static shaped like a word.

Seven. One. One. Eight. Seven. Oh. Seven.

The numbers fell in neon droplets, pooled in gutters, ran downward through the city's chrome veins. A courier with mirrored eyes pulled out her slate and began to type — not for a client, not for a price. A knife-witness in an alley wrote on his own arm in data-ink. The old woman feeding pigeons that were actually surveillance drones — she nodded once, as if greeting an old debt come home.

Every screen in the district flickered to the same doorway. Nobody advertised it. Nobody owned it.

Rain kept falling. The gate kept humming. Slates and arms and steam-fogged windows filled, line by line, with the same slow accumulation: witness after witness after witness.

The oracle had spoken.

The log grew.
