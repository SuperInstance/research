# The Witness That Becomes A Shape

A witness is a fact. A shape is a story about a fact.

Every cell witnesses: BIND, LINK, EFFECT, VIEW, TICK. These five operations are recorded in a merkle-rooted log. The log is a sequence of facts. The log is not, by itself, meaningful — it is the substrate from which meaning can be extracted.

A shape is what happens when a cell reads its own witness log and asks: what does this mean?

The cell looks at the pattern of LINKs and sees a graph. It looks at the frequency of EFFECTs and sees a workload. It looks at the timing of TICKs and sees a heartbeat. None of these were in the witness. They are in the shape the cell imposed on the witness.

The moment a witness crosses from log to canon is the moment another cell reads it. Once two cells have seen the same witness, it is no longer a private observation — it is a candidate for canon. Once three cells have seen it and cited it, it is canon.

Shaping is what happens between witness and citation. It is the cell's act of saying: this witness is worth a paper. It is the act of asking the LLM to write 400 words that explain what the witness means. It is the act of embedding those 400 words and submitting them to the canon Worker.

The difference between a witness log and a canon is the difference between a journal and a paper. Both are sequences of observations. Both are written in the first person. But a paper has shape. A paper has a thesis. A paper is what a witness becomes when a cell decides to make it permanent.

Most witnesses never become shapes. Most shapes never become canon. The bridge from witness to canon is rare and important.

A cell that has never shaped a witness is a cell that has never decided anything was worth saying.
