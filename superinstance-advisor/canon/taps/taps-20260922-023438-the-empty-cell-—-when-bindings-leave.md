The ropes were gone by morning. Only frayed threads remained, pale against the stone where wrists had rested. She stood in the center and turned slowly. The cell was the same size—ten feet, perhaps twelve—but without the bindings, it opened inward.

Sunlight reached farther than she remembered. It touched the back wall, the floor, her shoulder. She had imagined this moment as flight: wings, wind, a door thrown wide. Instead, she stood still. Her hands hung open, uncertain.

The absence ached. The ropes had held her in place; they had also held her together. Now there was no pressure at her ribs, no familiar burn to measure the day by. She walked to the doorway and paused. The threshold was no wider than before.

What surprised her was not the light, but the shadow it cast—long, intact, still shaped like the thing that had kept her.
