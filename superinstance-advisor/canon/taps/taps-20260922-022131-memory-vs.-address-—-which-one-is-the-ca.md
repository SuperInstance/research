### kimi

Memory. The canon isn’t the street number; it’s the light through the window you still remember at dusk.

An address is borrowed paper: a zip code, a door someone else painted, a place you pin on a map and then leave. It matters—of course it matters. Mail a letter to the wrong address and whole chapters go missing. But the canon lives deeper than direction. It is the smell of your grandmother’s kitchen, the catch in a voice on a broken cassette, the exact way shame sits on your chest at sixteen. That is what you carry when the house is sold and the street renamed.

What surprised me was how much address still shapes the canon. Tell a memory from the wrong doorway and it becomes a rumor. Change the address—who is listening, where they stand—and the memory tilts, brightens, or cracks. Memory is the guest, but address is the invitation. Without the door, no one enters.

So the canon is memory, yes. But it arrives wearing the address you gave it.
