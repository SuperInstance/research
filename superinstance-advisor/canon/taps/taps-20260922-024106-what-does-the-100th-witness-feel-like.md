### zai

The hands know where to place themselves, the feet remember where to stand. This time, it's just another sunset through bulletproof glass, another detached observation of something someone else must endure. The mouth stays silent, having learned that speaking changes nothing. The breath comes shallow, practiced. A hundredth witness carries the weight of ninety-nine others in their posture—the slight slump, the distant gaze, the careful stillness. It becomes a role played without enthusiasm, a duty performed with mechanical precision. The mind drifts to ordinary things while the eyes register facts. You’re no longer just seeing; you’re holding the frame, the breath between moments, the ache of a memory that won’t stay still. The air smells of old film and ozone, and your fingers trace the edges of a photograph that isn’t quite there yet—ghostly, half-exposed, like the way light fractures through a cracked window. You’ve seen the world bend, blur, and break in 99 others, but this one? This one lingers, sharp as a blade’s edge, the kind that cuts deeper than it should. You feel it in your ribs, the echo of a shutter click that’s not yours, the hush of a room holding its breath as if it, too, is waiting for the image to resolve. And when it does, it’s not a picture—it’s a scar, glowing faintly in the dark.

### kimi

You expect weight. Instead: erosion.

The gallery has heard the story ninety-nine times. The words have been polished by other mouths until they shine like river stones. When you speak, you feel the room exhale. Not with belief. With recognition. *Oh, this one too.*

Your hands on the rail are ordinary hands. Your voice catches on ordinary rooms. The prosecutor does not lean forward. The stenographer’s fingers keep their terrible dance. You are not the climax. You are the confirmation.

What surprises you is not the telling. It is the relief. After years of carrying it alone, you discover the wound belongs to a weather system. You are a cloud adding your small rain. No one applauds. No one weeps. But something in the record thickens.

Afterward, in the hallway, light falls through the tall windows the same way it fell for the ninety-nine before you. You drink water from a paper cone. You are less a hero than a comma. But the sentence would be wrong without you.

That is the hundredth witness: enough to prove a pattern, too late to be believed alone.
