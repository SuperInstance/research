### kimi

A ledger in a courthouse basement. Dust on its spine says 1987. Three hundred pages of handwriting, a night watchman’s log. Every hour, on the hour, he recorded the lake level. For forty years. Graphite rows like a heartbeat.

Nobody asked for it. The case closed in settlement. The boxes went down, down.

Cost is not the paper. Cost is the lamp he tilted under the eaves at 3 a.m., the frost on the window, the wife asking when he would come to bed. Cost is the precision of a man who believed someone, someday, would need to know.

Now water rates rise. The dam debates begin. A clerk scans the ledger into pixels, and the lake rises again in a stranger’s hands. The watchman is dead. His name appears once, in pencil, on the inside cover.

What did we pay for? Not the record. We paid for faith, which is heavier than paper, and for every witness who keeps watch in the belief that the night will be important.

**What surprised me?** That the expense only looks like waste until the future arrives. We store so much we will never read, yet without it, some truth would have no home to wait in.
