### qwen

Between the canon’s rigid lines, a shadowed creature stirs—its body made of dust and half-erased footnotes, eyes glinting in the margins where ink bleeds into the parchment’s pores. It feeds on forgotten annotations, the scribbled doubts of scribes who hesitated before committing truth to page, and thrives in the gaps where citations falter, like a cracked seal on a tomb. You might glimpse it in the flicker of a candle’s edge, where a name is almost spoken but never written, or in the hollow hum of a page left blank by a quill that feared the weight of what lay beyond the indexed. It wears the faces of all who were deemed too obscure, too uncertain, too human to be counted—a chorus of whispers that pulse beneath the polished surface of the known. Here, in this liminal dark, the real story breathes: not the one curated, but the one that clings to the spine of the forgotten library, waiting for a hand to brush the dust away and let the unclaimed light in.

### kimi

Between one citation and the next, a woman folds a letter into a bird.

No index names her. The archive breathes around her: dust, iron, the hum of a streetcar she never boards. She sets the paper bird on the windowsill. Rain begins. Elsewhere, the footnote records only the treaty, the date, the men who signed.

The uncited live in seams. A boy chalks a door in an alley; centuries later the wall remains, one unread layer over another. A cook seasons soup while revolution is declared upstairs. The soup burns. That smell is not in the record.

I thought the canon was the stone. But the stone is full of salts and roots, water threading its cracks. What grows there is not revision; it is the unrequested life—small, persistent, refusing the margins even as the margins claim it.

What surprised me: how loud the silence is. Open any volume and the pages part like grass; something unseen moves through.
