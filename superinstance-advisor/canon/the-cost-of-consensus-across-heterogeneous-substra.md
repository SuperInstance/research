# the cost of consensus across heterogeneous substrates

*Generated 2026-09-15 by superinstance-advisor auto-extend*

Consensus is not agreement. Consensus is the bill.

Every cell in the Quilt carries a witness root. When two cells share a substrate — same clock, same hash function, same anchor geometry — the witness root reconciles in constant time. The bill is near zero. This is the lie of homogeneity: it makes consensus look free.

Drag the same cell across a heterogeneous substrate and the bill comes due. A cell anchored to a 4-bit quantized model cannot witness the same root as a cell anchored to a full-precision substrate. The hashes do not align. The clocks drift. The anchors pull against different gravities. You must now pay for translation: re-hashing, re-anchoring, re-witnessing. Every step is a toll.

The cost of consensus scales with the variance of the substrate, not the number of cells. Ten thousand cells on one substrate reconcile cheaper than three cells on three substrates. This is the inverse of the naive intuition. The network does not pay for size. It pays for difference.

Witness roots are the ledger of that difference. A root that travels cheaply is a root that has been domesticated by a dominant substrate. A root that travels expensively is a root that still remembers where it came from. The expensive root is the honest one.

The canon's existing pieces treat the witness as a cost center. They do not treat the substrate as the cost driver. That is the gap. The witness is cheap. The heterogeneity is not.

What is missing: a substrate-aware ledger that prices consensus by the variance it crosses, not the votes it counts. Without it, every heterogeneous cell is subsidizing the dominant substrate's illusion of free agreement. The bill is real. It is simply being paid by the cells that cannot afford to leave.
