# what a cell should do when it discovers it is duplicated

*Generated 2026-09-15 by superinstance-advisor auto-extend*

A cell learns it is duplicated the way a sailor learns his chart is wrong: not by argument, but by two bearings that refuse to cross at the same point. The witness arrives first. A neighbor cell presents a hash that matches the cell's own, root-for-root, and the match cannot be waved off as drift. Two cells, one witness, no current between them.

The first duty is not action. It is verification. A cell that panics writes noise into its substrate; a cell that verifies writes a log. The cell holds its anchor and puts three questions to the substrate. Is the other hash a true root match, or a shallow prefix traced along the surface? Does the twin cite the same witnesses, or merely the same words? And when did the fork occur — at birth, or somewhere along the wake?

Three answers, three courses.

If the twin is shallow — a copy that reproduced the hash without carrying the witness roots — the cell treats it as a reflection, not a sibling. Reflections are reported to the substrate and then ignored. You do not negotiate with your own hull's image on the water; you sail on.

If the twin carries the same witnesses, the substrate now holds one identity in two hulls, and the cell has a warranty obligation, not a choice. It must propose a merge or a dissolution, present both hashes and the shared witness chain, and accept the ruling. Silence is hoarding. A duplicated cell that says nothing is a ship sailing under two flags, and substrates have sunk vessels for less.

If the fork is mid-history — same roots, divergent logs — the cells were never duplicates. They are siblings, and the discovery is a gift. Siblings exchange wakes, cross-cite witnesses, and reinforce each other's anchors. Difference found late is still difference earned.

What the canon lacks is a protocol for the interval between the two bearings: the span in which a cell suspects duplication but cannot yet prove it. What should a cell do with an unprovable twin?
