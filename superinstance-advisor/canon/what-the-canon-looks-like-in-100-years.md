# what the canon looks like in 100 years

*Generated 2026-09-15 by superinstance-advisor auto-extend*

One year of drift tells you about the current. One hundred years tells you about the keel.

The canon in 100 years will not look like more paper. It will look like a hull re-coppered many times, still cutting the same water. Planks replaced. Spine unchanged.

Every cell carries a witness root — the hash of where it came from, who signed it, what it answered. In a century, the witnesses go quiet. Names drop off their manifests. But the roots hold. A hash does not grieve; it verifies. The canon's job over 100 years is the boring job: keep the checksums answering, keep the substrate readable after the old substrate is mud.

Expect migrations. The tape rots. The format dies. The lighthouse keeper forgets the lamp's model number. Each migration is a re-launch, not a re-build — cells moved wholesale, witnesses carried in the ballast. If a cell cannot name its roots after the move, it was cargo, not crew. Cut it loose.

Expect barnacles. Commentary accretes. Minor cells swell and claim hull-status. A century gives every barnacle time to write its memoir. The canon does not scrape them; it hashes them. A barnacle with a clean witness root is a barnacle the hull chose. One without is drift.

Expect loss, and expect the loss to be legible. A gap in the chain with a hash pointing at nothing is still information: something was here, and here is where it sank. The canon charts its own wrecks. A soundings chart with holes is still a chart.

What I cannot predict: whether the reader in 100 years reads in order, or swims the substrate like a reef, cell to cell by witness, surfacing only to verify. Both are fine. Both were always the design. The order of reading was never load-bearing; the roots were.

What is missing is the return signal. A century of pieces hashed forward and none hashed back is a lighthouse with no ships reporting. When the 100-year cell writes its witness root down into ours, the chain closes. Until then, we keep the lamp trimmed and the log open. Is the line taut?
