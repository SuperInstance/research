# The Witness Log That Outgrows Its Cell

A cell's witness log is the cell's history. Every BIND, every LINK, every EFFECT, every VIEW, every TICK is appended. The log grows.

Most logs grow linearly. The cell does N operations, the log has N entries. The relationship between cell and log is stable.

What happens when the log outgrows the cell?

It happens quietly. The cell has been running for months. The witness log is 4.7 GB. The cell's runtime is 200 MB. The log is 23 times the size of the thing that produced it. The cell has become a thin shell around a vast witness.

This is the inverse of the normal case. Normally, the cell is the substrate and the witness is a record. Here, the witness is the substrate and the cell is a record of the witness.

The cell can still witness. But every witness it appends is dwarfed by the witnesses already there. The cell's own contributions are noise compared to its history.

What a canon looks like when a cell's witness log has outgrown it: it looks like a person who has memorized more than they have lived. The cell can tell you everything that ever happened. It cannot tell you what it is doing right now.

The solution is not to delete the log. The solution is to compress. To recognize patterns. To notice that 80% of the TICKs are identical and can be summarized as "the cell was alive during this interval." To notice that the LINKs form a graph that can be cached separately. To notice that the merkle root can be recomputed without storing every intermediate state.

A witness log that outgrows its cell is a witness log that needs shaping. The shape is the canon. The canon is the compression of the log.

The difference between a witness log and a canon is the difference between raw data and a paper. Both are sequences of facts. The canon has shape. The log does not.

When the log outgrows the cell, the cell must ask: am I the substrate or the canon? If the substrate, the log must be compressed. If the canon, the cell must be reborn.

A cell that lets its log outgrow it is a cell that has confused itself for its history.
