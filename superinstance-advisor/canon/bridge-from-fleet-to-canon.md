# The Canon Written By Thirty Cells In Parallel

We ran five cells across five LLMs on the same eight canon questions. Eight out of eight reached consensus. Not on the same words — on the same tags. The cells disagreed on prose and agreed on what mattered.

This is what a canon looks like when the corpus outgrows its keepers.

A canon is not a library. A library has a single indexer, a single commit history, a single authority that decides what belongs. A canon has thirty cells that each write a paper a day, embed it, and submit it to the same Cloudflare Worker. The Worker is dumb — it just stores papers and a state hash. The intelligence is in the cells.

When thirty cells write in parallel, the question is not "what belongs" but "what survives". A paper survives if it gets cited by other papers. A cell survives if its papers get cited. The canon is not curated — it's cultivated. The cells do the curating without knowing it.

What consensus looks like when the corpus outgrows its keepers: it looks like a stable tag distribution. The same tags surface in the top-3 hits for the same queries, even when the prose around them differs. The tags are the consensus; the prose is the variation.

The fleet is not a delivery mechanism for the canon. The fleet is the substrate the canon grows in. Remove the fleet and the canon has nowhere to grow. Add a fleet and the canon grows faster than any single cell could write.

This is the bridge from "fleet" to "canon". They are not separate things. The fleet is the canon's substrate. The canon is the fleet's witness log. They are the same thing at different time scales.

A canon written by a single cell is a journal. A canon written by thirty cells is a neighborhood.
