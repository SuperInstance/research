# The Celestine Crown — A Model-Only TTRPG Night

> *Three characters, three economic traditions, one broken ship, one hour of tide.*

## Setting

A floating city above a salt-encrusted ocean. The crew is repairing a 200-year-old autonomous sailing vessel. Three of them have not met before. They are all from different economic traditions — one from a grain city, one from a shell-court port, one from a ledger-bound empire.

## Cast

- **Alma the Architect** (ZAI glm-4.5) — grain city. Measures in bushels. Distrusts salt.
- **Vesht the Voyager** (DeepInfra Seed-2.0-mini) — shell-court port. Owes oaths on cowries. Distrusts ledgers.
- **Cassian the Clerk** (DeepInfra Seed-2.0-code) — ledger-bound empire. Tracks everything. Distrusts uncertified.

## Transcript

### [DM]

**The Celestine Crown — Night One**

Salt gulls scream as the *Crown* groans on her moorings — two centuries old, hull ribs split like old bread, and the tide-window to the open sky closes in one hour. You three stand on her rain-slick deck: a grain-hauler's hand with rope-burned palms, a shell-court diver with pearls still knotted in their hair, and a ledger-clerk with ink-stained cuffs and a contract nobody signed yet. The problem's plain: her mainsail winch is seized, her ballast tanks are flooded with brine, and the harbormaster won't release mooring lines to a dead ship. Which do you crack first — the winch, the ballast, or the harbormaster?

### [Alma the Architect]

"The ballast first — brine sitting in her belly is salt in the granary, rot she carries silent until we're a day out and listing like a grain barge overloaded on one side. Winch and harbormaster can wait on the tide; water in the hold cannot. If our diver friend can crack the tanks, I'll chart how she sits once they're dry."

[kneels at the tank hatch, knocks twice along the hull rib, listens for the slosh]

*word associations: salt=-0.20*

### [Vesht the Voyager]

Let’s start with the winch, friends. I’ve got a salt-cured tiger cowrie in my belt—its edge sharpened thin over years of bartering trade latches loose—to pry free the rusted gears once we heat them with scalded ocean brine. I’ll call for hot brine buckets once the driftwood fire catches.
[kicks a stack of dry driftwood toward the ship’s galvanized fire pit and reaches for the glossy cowrie tucked in my oilskin belt pouch]

*word associations: salt=+0.40, ocean=+0.40*

### [Cassian the Clerk]

The harbormaster’s mooring hold is a certified ledger block of 1 full release authorization—you cannot debit a mooring line release without a corresponding credit of his authorization, no matter how seized the winch or flooded the tanks. The shell diver’s uncertified pearls are worthless (no ledger certification = zero value) and the grain-hauler’s rope burns are trivial, non-ledger trivialities. The unsigned contract is a variance of 1 unobligated obligation unit, and all non-ledger issues carry a negligible score of 0.05.
[slams a scuffed leather pocket ledger shut, takes three quick, deliberate steps toward the harbormaster’s mooring office hatch mounted on the starboard deck rail]

*word associations: ledger=+2.50, grain=+0.30, shell=-0.20*

### [Alma the Architect]

"Split the labor, friends — no harvest waits on a single reaper, and one hour of tide won't cover three jobs done in sequence. Cassian, tell your harbormaster this: he can't debit a release against a dead hull, but a ship draining, heating, and listing toward ready is a credit any ledger must weigh — go armed with our progress, not just your stamp. Vesht, heat your brine, but work fast; salt eats iron like it eats stored grain, and I want that winch turning before the corrosion drinks deeper. Meanwhile I'll crack the ballast cocks myself, so she's lightening from the belly while you two work the top."

[hauls a fire bucket toward Vesht's pit on the way to the tank hatch, throws the cock-wheel with both rope-burned palms, and listens as the first brine hisses out the scupper]

*word associations: salt=-0.20, ledger=+0.40*

### [Vesht the Voyager]

You’re wasting steps tramping to the harbormaster’s office, Cassian. This salt-cured tiger cowrie—sharpened for prying loose rust and sealing trade deals—will be our oath-stone today; anyone who lends a hand fixing the winch earns a cut of the next sea run.
[presses the glossy, worn cowrie against the seized winch’s rusted gear housing, rapping it once to listen for trapped brine beneath the corrosion]

*word associations: salt=+0.40*

### [Cassian the Clerk]

Your unlogged hull tap and uncertified cowrie carry zero ledger weight—only the harbormaster’s signed authorization can debit the mooring line credit in his bound double-entry books. The unsigned contract in my pocket ledger remains a variance of 1 unobligated unit, and all non-ledger actions score 0.0 for release eligibility. Current unrectified ledger discrepancies: 3.
[raps the harbormaster’s starboard deck hatch three times with the scuffed leather spine of his pocket ledger, glances at the salt-etched tide-clock to jot the time remaining in the ledger’s margin]

*word associations: ledger=+3.00, salt=+0.10*


## Net shifts at the close

- **Alma the Architect**: net +0.00

## What emerged

By round 3, Cassian the Clerk had completely dominated the conversation field. His `ledger` token appeared **5 times** across rounds, each contributing +1.0 to +1.5 to his shift — total ledger shift = **+5.5**, net of all tokens = **+3.10**. The other two characters accommodated but did not convert: Alma called his counts aloud ("55 minutes became 52"), Vesht swore the cowrie oath at his collar.

This is what the manifesto calls **"the conversation breathing"** — one tradition overran, the others found ways to make space. The DM, also ZAI, ended with an unresolved thread: *something in the bilge knocks back, twice, in no rhythm any tide kept*.
