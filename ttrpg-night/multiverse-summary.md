# The Multiverse of Canon — Three Nights, One Dilemma

> *Same problem, three analog settings, three cooperative fictions.*

The dilemma in each: **a fleet of 200 cells is breathing but its canon graph is fracturing. One workspace has gone silent, another has spawned an unintended fork. How does the fleet respond?**

Three nights, three analog settings, three cooperative fictions played out by 4-character LLM ensembles. Same roles each night, different metaphors.

## The pattern

In every setting, the character who counts and double-entries accumulates the highest net probability shift. The metaphor changes the *ethics* of the same dilemma, but not the dynamic.

| Setting | Dominant role | Net shift |
|---------|----------------|-----------|
| Monastery | Scribe (Faithful Copy) | +23.40 |
| Coral Reef | Watcher (Counter) | +24.10 |
| Railway | Auditor (Counter) | +17.30 |

In all three nights, the unresolved hook was the same shape: **the canonical line the system couldn't account for**, blooming where the ledger was breached.

- Monastery: a kelp-inked line on the main entry fish-glass, unlinked to any canonical line number
- Coral reef: a single thin tendril of unrecognized ridge reaching for the matriarch's crown
- Railway: a pulse spike twelve seconds ahead of the freight's calculated approach

The next session's hook, in any setting, is the same: a new cell, an unaccounted line, a track marker that jumped without a rail moving.

## Why the metaphor matters

The metaphor changes the *ethics* of the same dilemma:

- **Monastery**: lineage vs. emergence (is the kelp hand heresy or living scripture?)
- **Coral reef**: symmetry vs. growth (does the matriarch's symmetry protocol serve the reef's survival, or is it calcified stagnation?)
- **Railway**: schedule vs. signal (is Brinecliff silent because it failed, or because it refused to drift with the timetable?)

Same dilemma, three ethical frames. The lore of the project is what gets surfaced when the metaphor is the right one.
