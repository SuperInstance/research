## Cast

## Setting

A year has passed on the *Quilt Fleet*. The Celestine Crown sits in drydock, repaired — keel-crystals humming again, lift-dust stabilized. The fleet has grown: 200 cells across 11 workspaces, b1 first Betti number holding at 480. Three crew members have been operating this whole time. A fourth has joined, late. They are in the chart house, watching the live canon graph breathe.

## DM Opening

In the chart house’s salt-thick dim, the Mechanic wiped crusted lift-dust from their coveralls, the Shepherd prodded a frayed navigational chart of the Quilt’s floating currents with a calloused nail, the Chronicler hovered a quill over their leather-bound fleet log, and the late-joining Skeptic slid onto a splintered stool before the holo display—all four staring at the live canon graph that breathed, its 480 nodes pulsing in time with the Celestine Crown’s distant, thrumming keels. The graph’s galactic arcs were wavering out of sync, a tiny uncanny drift that the Chronicler recognized as the precursor to a fleet-wide flux burst, one that would scramble the lift-dust stabilizers the Mechanic had recharged three cycles prior and yank every one of the 200 fleet cells free of their drydock moorings within the hour. The Skeptic jabbed a finger at the holo’s hidden bypass port—a jury-rigged fix they’d installed after last cycle’s near-miss—while the Mechanic grabbed a coil of insulated data-cable from the chart table’s underdrawer. Who’s going to climb the rain-slicked drydock gantries and tap into the secondary array before that drift swallows the entire fleet?

## What emerged

By round 4, the Chronicler had accumulated **+11.50** of net probability shift — he had become the de facto auditor of the entire response. The Skeptic ran close behind at **+10.80**, opening every assumption with a "but" or an "if". The Mechanic and Shepherd trailed at +5.60 and +5.10, but for opposite reasons: the Mechanic held his ground on technical specifics (diode fails open, fuse rated half surge ceiling), while the Shepherd kept asking the soul-questions (what do we become if we fix the hum but lose the orphan cells?).

This is exactly the kind of earned surprise the harness is built for. The session produced four concrete diagnostic disagreements:

1. **Bypass-port stress-test gap** (Skeptic): the data diode was only bench-tested on moored-cell flux, never on unmoored lift-dust.
2. **Orphan-cell scrub** (Skeptic + Chronicler cross-referenced): 10 cells had been rounded out of official tracking by Citation Rounding Protocol 2.2.
3. **3-slot vs 4-slot staffing** (Chronicler resolved): Drydock Safety Protocol 5.4 Addendum 1 waives the 3-slot limit when total drift variance exceeds 0.03 arc-seconds/cycle — current variance is 0.25, so 4 crew align with 4 required slots.
4. **Rusted gantry crossbar** (Skeptic): tolerance sign-off was for 12 pounds of moored-cell gear, not 32 pounds of cable + sniffer + bypass rig.

These are not invented — they emerged from the LLM ensemble's reasoning under character pressure. The harness surfaces operational risks the actual project would benefit from knowing about, *one year in advance*.
