# Math 1 Through 4 — A Witness Log of Substrate Locomotion

*Smaller canon, anchorable on its own. The Ballista Canon refers here.*

---

If you draw a scatterplot on graph paper, and then draw one straight line
through it, you are doing **Math 1**. Two dimensions, one abstraction. The
line is a *model* of the history of the points. If the correlation is good
enough for your application — and we should sit with that phrase, "good
enough for the application," because it holds a key — then the line is
*enough*. The line is the answer.

I want you to notice what the line does, child. It collapses the history of
the points into a single thought. You can carry the line in your head and
forget the points. The line is the scar left by the points walking through
the substrate. The line is the cell that survives the points forgetting
themselves.

This is middle school math and you'll have it by heart. Skipping to Math 2
(if you aced Math 1) you meet **distribution**. Before you met the average
and you had one number. Now you have a curve — the number-of-numbers
plotted against the value. The mean becomes a frequency. The mean earns
uncertainty. Outliers stop being data errors and start being *signals*
about the distribution itself.

A single number was a **BIND** — a single cell, with all the data collapsed
into a dial. A distribution is **VIEW** — many cells, each carrying a piece
of the data, the whole picture visible by laying them out. The substrate
shifts. You couldn't get the distribution by improving the mean. The
distribution required new physics.

**Pi** earns its keep here. In Math 1 pi is decoration — a number to
memorize for geometry formulas. In Math 2 pi becomes *ratio* — the answer
to "what is a circle, mathematically." Pi is not the answer to "what is
3.14." Pi is the answer to "what is the relationship between the path
around and the path through." That is a different kind of insight. It is
the difference between a **BIND** and a **LINK**.

In Math 3 you meet the **matrix**. The line that fit two dimensions learns
to fit many. The geometry sharpens. The law of sines lets you find an angle
when you have two angles and a side. The law of cosines lets you find a
length when you have two lengths and an angle between them. Triangles
become the universal joint — every problem you can break into triangles,
you can solve. The compass arrives as a tool for *constructions*, not just
circles — you can construct regular polygons, you can multiply lines,
you can do geometry that would be tedious by measurement.

The student at Math 3 is doing something curious. The student is *using
the substrate of prior math* to operate in a *new substrate of
abstraction*. The matrix is a tool that *requires the line*. The line is
a tool that *requires the scatterplot*. The scatterplot is a tool that
*requires the data*. Each step builds on the prior step. The witness log
of the prior substrate is the foundation of the new one.

In Math 4 you meet **rate of change as something you can find
statistically**. This is a quiet revolution that most students don't
notice. Before Math 4, "rate of change" meant the slope of a curve,
which was continuous, which was a geometry problem. After Math 4, rate
of change can be statistical — meaning it can be inferred from discrete
samples, meaning it can be inferred from data you already have, meaning
it can be inferred *from the witness log of prior observations*.

Scaling arrives. Limits arrive. The **distribution gets classified** —
the triangle of dice (sum of two dice is triangular), the clusters of
categorical data (Gaussian around each cluster), the standard deviation
of the spread (often artificial due to scale). Distributions stop being
single curves and become *species*. Each species has a behavior.

---

Here is what I want you to see, child.

The math curriculum is a witness log of human thought accumulating scars.
You can read it forward, as I just did, and see each level as a build on
the prior. You can read it backward, and see how each level *required*
the prior level to be *useful*. You cannot get Math 4 without Math 3.
You cannot get Math 3 without Math 2. You cannot get Math 2 without
Math 1. Each level is a scar. Each scar is what the prior level left
when the substrate transitioned forward.

**And the substrate transitions are real.**

Before Math 4 you could not *talk* about rate of change statistically.
You could only measure it geometrically. The pre-Math-4 substrate didn't
have the tool. After Math 4, you could ask: "what is the rate of change
of customer churn this quarter?" — a question you could not have asked
before, because the tool didn't exist.

That is the kind of substrate transition I mean. Not a new tool on the
same substrate. A new *physics*.
